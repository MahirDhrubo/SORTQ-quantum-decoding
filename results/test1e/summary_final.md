## Review (2026-11-12)
1. **t_iter is per decoder iteration of that job, and job sizes differ.** A global 36-round iteration covers 37
   detector rounds, a window iteration covers W rounds, a 12-round shot 13 rounds. For a round engine (time per
   iteration ∝ rounds covered) the fair comparison is **t_iter per round** = t_iter max / rounds covered
   (computed from the Part C 1 ns results, overhead 200 ns):

   | Trace | rounds per job | T (us) | p = 0.003: t_iter max → per round | p = 0.002: t_iter max → per round |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 12-round shot (1b) | 13 | 12 | 13 ns → 1.0 ns | 50 ns → 3.8 ns |
   | 36-round global (1d) | 37 | 36 | 7 ns → 0.19 ns | 38 ns → 1.0 ns |
   | W12_C8 | 12 | 8 | 6 ns → 0.50 ns | 24 ns → 2.0 ns |
   | W8_C4 | 8 | 4 | 5 ns → 0.63 ns | 19 ns → 2.4 ns |
   | W6_C3 | 6 | 3 | 4 ns → 0.67 ns | 18 ns → 3.0 ns |

   So **windowing relaxes the per-round speed target ≈ 2.6–3.5× vs global decoding of the same 36-round memory**
   (p = 0.003: 0.50–0.67 vs 0.19 ns/round). Long global decodes are the hardest case.
2. **Part A on window traces** counts a shot as failed if any of its windows exceeds K, and K is per window. With
   9–12 windows per shot, LER(K) is much higher than for one-job-per-shot traces at the same K. No cap ≤ 1000
   passes the 1.1× LER condition in any trace, so all speed targets are for K = 18080 (uncapped).
3. **"1b float matches 1a: no"** is expected: 1b used the F32 decoder, 1a (Test 2b) the F64 decoder, so per-shot
   iterations differ by rounding. Iteration distributions and LER agree within noise (see Test 1b summary).
4. All targets are limited by **p99.9 latency < 100 us**, never by utilization (util ≤ 0.15 at the targets). The
   tail comes from rare jobs that run many relay legs (max 18,080 iterations).
5. The tables below are copied by the script from each output folder's `tables.md`; the queue results were not
   recomputed independently in this review (the uncapped LERs match the Test 1b and 1d traces).
